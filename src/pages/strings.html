Strings

Strings are the only part of your racket that touches the ball. The right string and tension can change how a racket plays more than switching rackets, and it costs a fraction of the price. Answer seven quick questions below to find a setup that suits your game.

In one line

The short answer

If you only read one thing, start with the setup that matches you, then adjust tension.

Beginner or casual player

Synthetic gut, 16 gauge

24–25 kg (53–55 lbs). Cheap, comfortable, forgiving. Change it when it feels dead or breaks.

Club player, arm is fine

Soft co-poly or a hybrid

Poly 22–24 kg (48–53 lbs), or poly mains with multifilament crosses for spin with comfort.

Sore elbow or wrist

Multifilament or natural gut

24–26 kg (53–57 lbs). The softest materials; skip full polyester until the arm settles.

Big topspin hitter who breaks strings

Shaped polyester, 16 gauge

22–24 kg (48–53 lbs). Durable and spin-friendly. Restring every 15–20 hours to keep it lively.

The basics

Why strings matter

They hit the ball

The ball sits on the strings for only a few thousandths of a second, but in that time the stringbed decides how deep the ball goes, how much it spins, and what you feel in your hand.

Tension changes every shot

Looser strings stretch more and throw the ball deeper with less effort. Tighter strings give a lower, more controlled ball but less power. Even 2 kg is easy to feel.

They go dead before they break

Every string loses tension from the day it is strung. If the ball starts flying long or the racket feels harsh and lifeless, the strings are worn out, even if none has broken.

Your arm feels it

Stiff polyester at high tension is a common reason club players get elbow and wrist pain. A softer string at a lower tension takes much of the shock off your arm.

By player

Which string suits your game?

Your swing and your arm matter more than what the pros use.

Short, compact swing

You need the string to help with depth. Multifilament or synthetic gut at a mid tension gives free power. Stiff poly will feel dead and land short.

Long, fast swing with topspin

Polyester controls the extra power and lets the ball dip. Shaped polys add a little bite; an open 16×19 pattern adds more.

Net player and doubles

Touch and feel matter more than spin. Multifilament, gut, or a hybrid with soft crosses gives control on volleys without harshness.

Arm, elbow or shoulder pain

Choose multifilament or natural gut and drop tension 1–2 kg. If you keep poly, use the softest co-poly and restring it before it goes dead.

Junior or new player

Synthetic gut. It is cheap enough to restring often and soft enough for developing arms.

You break strings every few weeks

Go thicker (16 or 15L), use polyester in the mains, or try a denser 18×20 racket pattern.

Avoid these

Common mistakes

Copying the pros

Tour players use stiff poly because they swing very fast, restring every match and have trainers looking after their arms. At club swing speeds, full poly often feels dead and hurts.

Waiting for a break

If you rarely break strings, they are probably long dead. A good rule: restring as many times a year as you play each week, and poly far more often.

Tightening for control

Balls flying long are often a sign of old strings, not low tension. Restring at the same tension first. Going much tighter costs power and is harder on the arm.

Playing through pain

At the first sign of elbow pain, switch to multifilament or gut and drop 1–2 kg. If the pain stays, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Beyond material

Shape, texture and hybrids

Round strings

Smooth and consistent. Most multifilaments, guts and many tour polys (Luxilon ALU Power, Yonex Poly Tour Pro) are round.

Shaped strings

Octagonal, hexagonal or square polys (Babolat RPM Blast, Head Lynx Tour, Solinco Hyper-G) grip the ball slightly more. Most spin still comes from how freely the strings slide and snap back.

Mains and crosses

Mains run top to bottom and do most of the work, so they break first. Crosses run side to side and shape comfort and feel.

Hybrid setups

Poly mains with multi or gut crosses: spin and durability with a softer feel. Gut mains with poly crosses (Federer’s setup): maximum feel with better durability than full gut.

Upkeep

When to restring

Strings lose tension from the moment they are strung, even if they never break.

The yearly rule

Restring as many times a year as you play per week. Play twice a week, restring about twice a year.

The polyester rule

Poly goes dead before it breaks. Many poly users restring every 15–20 hours of play, or about monthly for regular players.

Signs it is time

Strings stop snapping back into place, the ball flies long or feels “boardy”, notches appear where strings cross, or your arm starts to ache.
